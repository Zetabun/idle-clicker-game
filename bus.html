LIVE GPS means the latest bus position was reported within the last two minutes. ETA confidence is stricter and still uses the vehicle's normal 30 to 75 second reporting cadence. GPS DELAYED means the last confirmed report is two to four minutes old; Kerbside holds the row briefly rather than letting the bus flicker out. GPS SIGNAL LOST means the operator stopped reporting a bus it had already confirmed, so the row is held without a countdown until it expires. Later timetable departures stay scheduled until a matching vehicle begins service. Confidence runs verified, where journey or timetable evidence confirms this bus serves the stop, then likely, where route and direction evidence agree but the journey is not fully matched, then rough, where evidence is limited and the ETA is approximate. Tap a matched live bus for its ordered stop progress; a map route is drawn only when the national GTFS feed supplies an official shape for that exact journey. Roadworks and closures on the routes that call here appear above the board, matched by exact stop code and filtered to what is running now.

GPS supported. The map uses operator vehicle-position snapshots from the BODS live feed. Between reports, Kerbside shows a short estimated glide based on recent confirmed movement; stop matching and ETAs continue to use confirmed GPS records. Positions over two minutes old are labelled GPS delayed and retained only until four minutes old so irregular operator updates do not make an approaching bus vanish immediately. ETA confidence still follows each vehicle's normal 30 to 75 second reporting cadence. Kerbside periodically scans up to 18 km around the selected stop, but buses beyond the normal nearby area appear only when their exact live journey is scheduled to call there. It also follows ordered timetable journeys upstream, checking at most three additional route-corridor areas once per minute and retaining only exact live journeys that have not passed this stop.

Published beside the app. Roadworks and closures come from the Department for Transport SIRI-SX archive and are matched to a route by exact ATCO stop code, never by line name, because most line names are shared by several operators nationally. Only situations inside their published validity window are counted, so the figure above is smaller than the number in the raw archive. Coverage follows what each local transport authority chooses to publish: it is strongest across the large city regions, absent in many areas, and does not include London. Stop details — which way a bus faces, the street, and whether the stop is hail and ride — come from the national NaPTAN register and cover active bus stops across Great Britain.
